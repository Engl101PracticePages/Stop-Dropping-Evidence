Stop Dropping Evidence

Evidence does not explain itself. Practice moving from a quote or paraphrase into real analysis, so your paragraph does more than repeat the source.

Return to Toolkit Work Hub

Step 1

Mini lesson: evidence is not analysis

The problem is not using evidence. The problem is dropping in a quote or paraphrase and then writing a sentence that only repeats the same information.

1

This means...

Translate the evidence. What is the source actually showing us?

2

This matters because...

Explain the “so what.” Why should the reader care about this evidence?

3

This connects to my claim by...

Link the evidence back to your paragraph’s main idea.

Step 2

Example: nursing

Notice how the analysis does not just repeat the evidence. It explains what the evidence means, why it matters, and how it connects back to the claim.

Evidence

Paraphrase + citation

The Bureau of Labor Statistics describes registered nurses as providing and coordinating patient care, educating patients, and administering treatments. (BLS)

Analysis

3-sentence rule

This means the role involves both medical tasks and communication responsibilities, not just “helping people.”

This matters because the daily work includes emotional labor and high responsibility, which can affect stress and job satisfaction.

This connects to my claim by showing that whether nursing is a “good fit” depends on the kind of work a person wants to do, not only job growth.

Remember: you are allowed to say a career is “rewarding” or “great,” but you need evidence and explanation. You cannot just drop the word and move on.

Step 3

Practice: repetition or analysis?

Each item includes evidence and one after-evidence sentence. Decide whether the after-evidence sentence is repetition or analysis.

Choose one:
  • R: repetition, because it mostly restates the evidence
  • A: analysis, because it explains meaning, importance, connection, or limitation

Step 4

Revise your draft analysis

Use your actual draft and your actual in-text citation. Either a quote or a paraphrase is fine.

E) Rewrite using the 3-sentence rule

Step 5

Choose one analysis move

Pick one move and add one more sentence. This is the “go deeper” part.

Tip: your “analysis” should add meaning, not just repeat the evidence. Add a reason, implication, limitation, comparison, definition, or connection to your claim.

Common mistake to avoid: If your analysis sentence could be replaced with “This shows ___” and nothing changes, it is probably repetition.
